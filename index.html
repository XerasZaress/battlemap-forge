Water & lava

Exports are still images, so the current shows as caustics, streaks and cracked crust rather than motion. The preview animates so you can aim the flow. Lava creeps at a fraction of the water speed, the way molten rock should.

Weather & time of day

Weather is seeded from the map, so the same storm falls in the same places every time you export. It is painted into the image — a VTT that lights the scene itself will still light it through the fog.

Turn baked lighting off if your VTT does its own dynamic lighting — the Universal VTT export carries the light sources either way.

Trace an existing map

Load any map image (Czepeku, Patreon art, a scan) and paint walls over it, then export a Universal VTT with line-of-sight built in.

Adjust until the on-screen grid lines up with the art — this matters most, because everything below keys off it.

Automatic tracing

Works best on maps with dark walls around lit floors. Raise sensitivity to catch faint walls (and more noise), lower it to keep only strong ones. Then fix up by hand with the Partition tool and cut doorways with Door.

Taking the map to a VTT

  • Roll20 / Owlbear / Alchemy Upload the PNG. Set the scene to 30 × 22 squares.
  • Foundry Drop the PNG in your world's Data folder, then import the scene JSON — walls, doors and lights come with it. Or use the Universal VTT file with the Universal Battlemap Importer module.
  • Fantasy Grounds Unity Import the .dd2vtt directly (Import > Universal VTT).
  • Arkenforge / Dungeon Alchemist The .dd2vtt is the same format.

Tool options

Partitions sit on the lines between squares, so a wall never eats a 5 ft square. With the Door tool, click a partition to cut a doorway — click again for a secret door.

Prefab rooms

Pick a room, then click the map to stamp it. Rooms placed side by side share the wall between them.

Make your own

Drag a rectangle on the map. Its terrain, partitions and furniture become a reusable prefab.

Room

Drag the room on the map to move it. Lifting a room restores whatever it was covering.

Layers

The order things are drawn in, top of the stack first. Drag a row to move it. Click a row to make it the one new work lands on.

Layer

Hiding a layer takes it out of the map entirely: its props stop blocking sight and its torches stop giving light, in the editor and in every export. That is how you get a players' copy of a map with the GM's notes on it.

Labels

Pick the Label tool and click the map to write on it. Click an existing label with Select or Label to edit it.

Labels are drawn over everything, including fog and baked lighting, and burn into the exported image. Curve bends the line the letters sit on — the sign decides which way it bows.
